Profile

About

I am a Graduate Teaching and Research Assistant in the Department of Construction Technology and Management at KNUST, and a member of the department's Building Science, Engineering and Materials research team. I work with Prof. Kofi Agyekum and Prof. Emmanuel Adinyira on sustainable construction, circularity, digital product passports and the use of AI in the built environment.

Much of my empirical work is set in Ghana, where I study why practices such as materials passports, design for adaptability and design for greater efficiency are, or are not, taken up by professionals. I combine questionnaire surveys and structural equation modelling with systematic reviews, and I am building skills in ontology engineering and Python for material passport data.

Alongside research I lead tutorials in Building Science, Strength of Materials and Research Methods, and help supervise undergraduate and postgraduate research projects.

Projects

Current and recent work

Active

Digital product passports for sustainable construction

A carbon-centric approach to material passports that integrates BIM, blockchain and carbon databases, so that material data stays traceable and trustworthy across a building's lifecycle. The work includes an OWL ontology built in Protégé and smart contract prototypes on Hyperledger Fabric.

DPPsOntologyBlockchainCarbon
In development

A decision-triggered ontology extension for carbon governance in digital product passports

A design science study that extends an existing passport ontology with decision-record logic, so that when a carbon value is revised (for example, a new EPD for a blended cement) a governed decision is raised, reviewed and recorded. Competency questions and SPARQL queries test whether the trigger behaves for carbon data as it does for structural data.

MPhil thesis: design for greater efficiency in Ghana

A PLS-SEM study of 238 design professionals drawing on Diffusion of Innovations and the Theory of Planned Behaviour. Perceived importance was the strongest driver of implementation (β = 0.394, p < .001), and the model explained 41.4% of the variance.
